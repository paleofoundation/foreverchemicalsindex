# Influence of Talc in Polypropylene on Total Fluorine Measurements Used as an Indicator of Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances (PFAS)

## Summary

Laboratory material and method study. Inorganic fluorine in talc contributes to the total fluorine signal in polypropylene. This is supportive analytical context, not measured PFAS occurrence in commercial plastic tubs. Confidence HOLD.

## Methods (brief)

Virgin polypropylene was compounded with talc and analyzed as cryomilled powder and molded pucks. Near-infrared spectroscopy was combined with oxidative pyrohydrolytic combustion ion chromatography to investigate inorganic filler contributions. The instrument quantified fluoride after combustion (PDF pp. 2-4). This is total fluorine, not a targeted LC-MS/MS measurement or a direct total organic fluorine measurement. No EPA targeted method is claimed.

Quantitation uses sodium-fluoride calibration. Printed method limits use a target sample mass of 25 mg (PDF p. 4, Quantitation). The printed instrumental and method units are retained without deriving a conversion. The source says all formulation measurements were above the method LOQ (PDF p. 5, Combustion Ion Chromatography). No non-detect product result is reported.

## Limitations

Laboratory formulation results do not establish occurrence or migration from a commercial tub. No plot digitization, organic/inorganic subtraction, regulatory limit or compliance conclusion is imported. Supplementary spectra and computational appendices are not staged. The source describes additive interference; it does not establish that talc fluorine is PFAS.
